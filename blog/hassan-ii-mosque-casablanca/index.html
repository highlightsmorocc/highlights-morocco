Hassan II Mosque, Casablanca

Tickets, guided tour times, dress code, history and tips to visit the grandest mosque in Morocco, on the Atlantic coast.

The short answer

The Hassan II Mosque in Casablanca is the largest mosque in Morocco, with a minaret about 200 metres tall, standing on the Atlantic coast. It is one of the few mosques in Morocco that non-Muslims can enter, and only on a guided tour with a ticket, which has cost around 130 dirhams for foreign adults in recent years. The tour lasts about 45 to 60 minutes and runs at set times each day, so check the schedule. Dress modestly, take off your shoes at the entrance and go in the morning for the best light and the fewest crowds.

The Hassan II Mosque is the best reason to stop in Casablanca. It is also the one sight that many travellers search for before they arrive, with questions about tickets, opening hours, the tour and what to wear. This guide answers them, explains the history and design, and shows how to fit the visit into a day or a longer trip. Prices and hours change, so use them as a guide and confirm on the day. When you want help, message us on WhatsApp and we plan it for you.

History and design of the Hassan II Mosque

The mosque was ordered by King Hassan II and designed by the French architect Michel Pinseau. Construction began in 1986 and the mosque opened in 1993. Thousands of Moroccan craftsmen worked on it, and the result is a mix of traditional Moroccan decoration and modern engineering. Zellige tiles, carved plaster, painted cedar and marble are used throughout, and much of the material comes from Morocco.

The setting is part of the story. The mosque stands on a platform at the edge of the Atlantic Ocean, and part of the building extends over the water. The prayer hall can hold about 25,000 worshippers, and the esplanade around it can hold many more. The hall also has a retractable roof that can open towards the sky, and a lower level with a hammam and ablution rooms that you can see on the tour.

Where to buyAt the official ticket office, so avoid street sellers

The mosque is a place of prayer, so tours stop during prayer times and the schedule can change at short notice. Arrive early, carry some cash in dirhams and ask the ticket office for the next tour in English, French or another language. Read our guide to money, currency and tipping in Morocco before you go.

How to get to the Hassan II Mosque

  • From Casablanca city centre: a short taxi ride, usually 10 to 15 minutes depending on traffic.
  • From Casablanca airport: about 30 km, with a train to the city and a taxi to the mosque, or a private transfer.
  • From Marrakech: about 240 km, either by road or by train, usually around three hours each way.
  • From Rabat: about 90 km, around one hour by road, so it fits a day trip or a stop on the way.
  • On foot: the mosque is near the sea front and the Corniche, and you can walk the esplanade freely.

A private driver is the easiest way to combine the mosque with other stops, because you avoid waiting for taxis and train times. Read our guide to where Morocco is if you want to see how the cities connect.

Visitor tips for the Hassan II Mosque

  • Go in the morning: tours are quieter and the light is better for photos.
  • Dress modestly: read our Moroccan clothing guide.
  • Wear shoes that come off easily: you remove them to enter.
  • Check the tour times: they can change on Fridays and during holidays.
  • Respect the space: keep your voice low and ask before you take photos of people.
  • Stay for sunset: the minaret and the sea look beautiful at dusk, and the laser beam shines at night.
  • Mind your belongings: Casablanca is a big city, so keep a bag in front of you. See is Morocco safe for tourists.

Is the Hassan II Mosque worth visiting?

Yes. Casablanca is a modern, busy business city, and the mosque is the one place that feels truly monumental and different from the other cities in Morocco. Even travellers who do not usually visit religious buildings enjoy the scale, the craft and the ocean setting. The guided tour adds the history and the details that you would miss on your own, so we recommend it over a walk around the outside only.

Time of dayWhat it is like
MorningQuiet, good light, the best time for the guided tour
MiddayBusy with groups and strong sun on the esplanade
Late afternoonSoft light and a good time to walk the sea front
Dusk and nightThe minaret is lit and the laser beam shines towards Mecca

If you can only choose one moment, take the tour in the morning and come back to the esplanade near sunset. The two visits feel completely different, and the sea air makes the evening walk very pleasant.

Near the Hassan II Mosque: what else to see

  • The Corniche: the seafront promenade with cafes and beaches.
  • The old medina: a smaller, calmer medina than Marrakech or Fes.
  • The Habous quarter: a 20th-century district with arcades, bookshops and pastry shops.
  • Mohammed V Square and the art deco centre: colonial-era buildings downtown.
  • Rick's Cafe: a restaurant inspired by the film Casablanca.

Plan your day around the mosque

Most visitors spend half a day in Casablanca. Visit the mosque in the morning, walk the esplanade and the Corniche, have lunch by the sea and then explore the old medina or the Habous quarter. If you are on a longer trip, Casablanca fits well at the start or the end, because the main airport is close. If you arrive late or leave early, ask us for a private transfer so that you do not lose time in taxis or on trains, and so that the mosque visit fits comfortably between your flights and your other stops.

Hassan II Mosque: frequently asked questions

What is the Hassan II Mosque?

The Hassan II Mosque is the largest mosque in Morocco and one of the largest in the world. It stands on the Atlantic coast of Casablanca, with a minaret about 200 metres high, a huge prayer hall, a large esplanade and a design that mixes Moroccan craft with modern engineering. It opened in 1993.

Can non-Muslims visit the Hassan II Mosque?

Yes. The Hassan II Mosque is one of the few mosques in Morocco that non-Muslims can enter, but only on a guided tour with a ticket. Tours run at set times through the day and the programme can change on Fridays and during prayer times, so check the schedule on the day.

How much are Hassan II Mosque tickets?

Foreign adults usually pay around 130 dirhams for the guided tour, with lower prices for children and students. Prices and rules can change, so check the amount at the ticket office and pay only at the official counter. Our team can help you plan your visit.

What are the Hassan II Mosque opening hours?

The mosque offers guided visits for non-Muslims in the morning and through the day, usually every day, with some changes on Fridays and during religious holidays. Because times vary through the year, check the latest schedule or ask us before you go.

How long does the Hassan II Mosque tour take?

A guided visit usually lasts about 45 minutes to one hour. Allow another 30 to 60 minutes to walk around the esplanade and the sea front, and to take photos from outside.

What should I wear to the Hassan II Mosque?

Wear modest clothes that cover shoulders, chest and knees. Women do not have to cover their hair for the tour, but a light scarf can be useful. You take your shoes off to enter, so wear shoes that are easy to remove, and bring socks if you prefer.

Can I take photos inside the mosque?

Photos are usually allowed in many parts of the visit, but the rules can change and the guide will tell you where to take pictures. Be respectful of people who are praying and avoid flash.

How tall is the minaret?

The minaret of the Hassan II Mosque is about 200 metres tall, which makes it one of the tallest in the world. At night a laser beam shines from the top towards Mecca.

How do I get to the Hassan II Mosque?

The mosque is on the sea front of Casablanca, a short taxi ride from the city centre. From Casablanca airport it is about 30 km, and from Marrakech it is a drive or a train ride of around three hours. Our team can arrange a private transfer.

What else can I see near the Hassan II Mosque?

Nearby you can walk the Corniche, the old medina, Mohammed V Square and the Habous quarter. See our guide to things to do in Casablanca.
