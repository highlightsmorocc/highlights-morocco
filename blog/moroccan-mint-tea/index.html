Moroccan mint tea: recipe and tradition

How to make Moroccan mint tea, the story behind the ritual, the teapot, the manners of the table and where to drink it on your trip.

The short answer

Moroccan mint tea is a sweet tea made with gunpowder green tea, fresh mint and sugar, served hot in small glasses from a silver-plated teapot. It is the national drink of Morocco and the first sign of welcome in a home, shop or camp. To make it, rinse a spoonful of gunpowder tea, add fresh mint and sugar to the pot, pour in boiling water, steep for a few minutes and pour from a height to make foam. Three glasses is the traditional offer, and accepting the first one is the polite way to respond.

If you have been to Morocco, you already know the moment: someone sits you down, brings a tray and pours a glass of hot, sweet, fragrant tea before you have said a word. This guide explains what Moroccan mint tea is, how to make it at home, what the ritual means, how to behave when you are offered a glass and where to drink and buy it on your trip. It is one of the simplest and most memorable parts of Moroccan culture. When you want help planning your trip, message us on WhatsApp.

The story of Moroccan mint tea

Green tea came to Morocco in the 18th and 19th centuries through trade, and over time Moroccans added fresh mint and a lot of sugar, creating a drink that is now part of everyday life. Tea is served at home, in shops, at business meetings, in cafes and in the desert, and offering it is a sign of respect. People often call it Berber whisky, as a joke about a drink that is strong and sweet but contains no alcohol. In Morocco, tea is not only a drink: it is the way people say hello, close a deal and spend time together.

How to make Moroccan mint tea

  • Rinse the tea: put a tablespoon of gunpowder tea in the pot, add a little boiling water, swirl for about 30 seconds and pour that water away. This removes bitterness.
  • Add mint and sugar: push a large bunch of fresh mint into the pot and add the sugar.
  • Add boiling water: pour in about a litre of freshly boiled water.
  • Steep: let it rest for three to five minutes, or heat it gently on the stove for a stronger flavour.
  • Mix: pour a glass and return it to the pot two or three times so that the sugar and the mint blend.
  • Serve from a height: pour into the glasses from a height to cool the tea and create foam.

The measurements are a guide. Many families adjust the sugar and the mint by taste, and the first glass is often the lightest and the last the strongest. If you are in Morocco, you can ask your host how they like it or join a cooking class to learn it in person. See our Moroccan cooking class in Marrakech.

The ritual and the etiquette of Moroccan tea

Tea is usually prepared and served by the host or the head of the family. The pot is held high, the tea falls into the glass and a layer of foam rises on top. The first glass is offered to the guest, and the host serves everyone before refilling. A well-known saying about the three glasses describes the first as gentle, the second as strong and the third as bitter, which reflects how the taste changes as the tea steeps.

  • Accept the first glass: it is a sign of respect and friendship.
  • Use your right hand: when you take the glass, as is the local custom.
  • Sip slowly: the tea is very hot, so hold the glass by the rim or the base.
  • Enjoy the sweetness: if it is too sweet, you can ask for less sugar when you are ordering in a cafe.
  • Thank your host: a simple thank you and a smile are enough.
  • Do not rush: tea is a moment to sit and talk.

Different regions and families have their own habits, and cafes often offer a few versions. In winter you may be offered a tea with local herbs, and in summer many people prefer it lighter. Ask what is available and try a few.

In a Moroccan home, tea is almost always served with something to eat, from a plate of cookies to a full breakfast. If you are invited, take a small gift, such as pastries, and enjoy the slow pace. Read our Moroccan food guide for the dishes that come with the tea.

Tips for making Moroccan mint tea at home

  • Use fresh mint: dried mint gives a very different taste.
  • Rinse the tea first: it makes the tea clearer and less bitter.
  • Add the sugar in the pot: so that it dissolves while the tea steeps.
  • Do not boil the tea for too long: a short steep keeps the flavour fresh.
  • Pour from a height: it mixes the tea and makes foam, but be careful with hot water.
  • Use small glasses: they keep the tea hot and make it feel like Morocco.

What to buy: teapots, glasses and tea

If you want to take the tradition home, you can buy a teapot, a tray, small glasses and a bag of gunpowder tea in any souk. Look at the weight and the finish of the teapot, check that the lid fits and ask about the metal. Compare prices in a few shops, bargain politely and agree on the price before you pay. For tips on shopping in the souks, read our guide to Moroccan rugs and our money and tipping guide.

Tips for travellers

  • Tea is not always free: in cafes you pay, but in shops and homes it is a gesture of welcome.
  • Watch the sugar: ask for less if you prefer.
  • A glass of tea in a shop does not oblige you to buy, but be polite and thank the seller.
  • Drink bottled or boiled water: read is Morocco safe for tourists for health tips.
  • Learn a few words: shukran means thank you.
  • Pack light: a teapot and glasses are fragile, so wrap them well. See our Moroccan clothing guide and our Moroccan food guide.

Moroccan mint tea: frequently asked questions

What is Moroccan mint tea?

Moroccan mint tea is a sweet tea made from Chinese gunpowder green tea, fresh mint and sugar, served hot in small glasses from a metal teapot. It is the national drink of Morocco and a sign of welcome and hospitality.

How do you make Moroccan mint tea?

Rinse a tablespoon of gunpowder green tea with a little boiling water and pour that water away. Add a large bunch of fresh mint and sugar to the teapot, pour in boiling water and let it steep for a few minutes. Pour a glass, return it to the pot to mix, then serve from a height.

Is Moroccan mint tea made with spearmint or peppermint?

The classic version uses fresh spearmint, often called nana mint, although many people search for Moroccan peppermint tea. You can use peppermint if you cannot find spearmint, but the taste will be a little sharper.

What kind of tea is used in Moroccan mint tea?

Gunpowder green tea, which has leaves rolled into small pellets that open as they steep. It gives a strong, slightly smoky base that balances the sugar and the mint.

Why is Moroccan tea poured from a height?

Pouring from a height cools the tea a little, mixes the sugar and creates a layer of foam on top. It is also part of the ritual and a sign of skill and hospitality.

How many glasses of tea are served?

Three is the traditional number, and many hosts offer a second and a third glass. Accepting at least the first glass is a polite way to respond to hospitality.

Is it rude to refuse Moroccan tea?

Politely accepting the first glass is a good rule. If you do not want more, you can thank your host and say that you are full. Do not worry if you cannot finish, but try not to refuse more than once or twice.

What is a Moroccan teapot called?

The typical teapot is a silver-plated metal pot called a berrad, with a long spout, a lid and a handle. It sits on a tray with small decorated glasses.

Where can I drink Moroccan mint tea in Morocco?

In cafes in every city, on riad terraces, in spice shops and souks, in desert camps and in Berber villages in the Atlas. Try it in the Kasbah of the Udayas in Rabat and around the squares of Marrakech and Fes.

Can I buy a Moroccan teapot and glasses?

Yes. Souks sell teapots, trays and glasses in many styles. Ask the seller about the metal and the quality, compare a few shops and agree on the price before you buy. Read our guide to Moroccan rugs for tips on shopping in the souks.
