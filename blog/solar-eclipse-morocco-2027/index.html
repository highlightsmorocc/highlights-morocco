Solar eclipse 2027 in Morocco

The total solar eclipse of 2 August 2027 crosses northern Morocco. Where the path of totality passes, how long it lasts, where to watch and how to travel safely.

The short answer

The total solar eclipse of 2 August 2027 crosses the Strait of Gibraltar and the far north of Morocco, including Tangier, where totality lasts about 4 minutes and 50 seconds, according to most eclipse guides. It happens in the morning, and Morocco uses GMT+1 in August. The rest of the country, including Marrakech, sees a partial eclipse. To see totality, you must be inside the narrow path of totality, so choose a base in or near Tangier and Tetouan, book early, and use certified eclipse glasses for every phase except the short total phase.

A total solar eclipse is one of the most impressive natural events you can see. In the middle of the morning the sky darkens, the temperature drops, the birds go quiet and a black disc with a glowing white crown appears where the Sun was. Most people never see one. In 2027 the path of totality crosses northern Morocco, which makes it one of the best eclipse trips in the world, and one that you can combine with the blue town of Chefchaouen, the medina of Fes and the Sahara. This guide explains the facts and how to plan. When you want help, message us on WhatsApp and we plan it for you.

What is a solar eclipse?

A solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes between the Sun and the Earth and blocks the light of the Sun. The Moon is much smaller than the Sun, but it is also much closer, so from the Earth the two look almost the same size. When they line up, the Moon can cover the whole Sun for a few minutes.

TypeWhat you seeHow common
Total eclipseThe Moon covers the whole Sun, the sky becomes dark and the corona appearsRare in any one place
Annular eclipseThe Moon is a little farther away, so a bright ring of the Sun remainsLess rare
Partial eclipseThe Moon covers only part of the Sun, like a biteThe most common
Hybrid eclipseThe eclipse changes between annular and total along its pathRare

Solar eclipse vs lunar eclipse. In a lunar eclipse the Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon, and the shadow of the Earth falls on the Moon. A lunar eclipse is visible from the whole night side of the planet and is safe to watch with your eyes. A solar eclipse is visible only along a narrow track, and it needs eye protection.

This eclipse is often called the "eclipse of the century" because totality is so long. For the longest totality you need to go to Egypt, but Tangier and the north of Morocco give you almost five minutes, which is far longer than most eclipses.

The path of totality across Morocco

The path of totality is the narrow strip where the Moon completely covers the Sun. The 2027 path starts in the Atlantic, crosses southern Spain and the Strait of Gibraltar and reaches Morocco at the far north. It then continues east over Algeria, Tunisia, Libya and Egypt. On the ground the path is roughly 250 kilometres wide, so some places are inside it and others are only a short drive away.

  • Inside the path: Tangier and the area around Tetouan, on the northern coast of Morocco.
  • Near the edge: towns in the Rif, such as Chefchaouen, and parts of the northern Mediterranean coast. Check the exact edge on an official map before you decide.
  • Outside the path: Rabat, Casablanca, Fes, Marrakech and the south, which see a partial eclipse.

The edge of the path is sharp. A few kilometres can be the difference between a few seconds of totality and none. If totality is your goal, stay near the centre line, not at the edge.

Date and time of the eclipse in Morocco

The eclipse takes place on 2 August 2027, in the morning. The partial phase starts about an hour before totality, the total phase lasts a few minutes and the partial phase continues for about an hour afterwards. Most guides give the greatest eclipse in Tangier at around 9:45 local time, but always confirm the exact times for your location on an eclipse timetable.

In August, Morocco uses GMT+1, which is the same as Madrid in winter and one hour behind Madrid in summer. If you plan to follow the eclipse live with friends abroad, check our live clock and guide to what time it is in Morocco now.

What you will see during the eclipse

StageWhat happens
First contactThe Moon touches the edge of the Sun and a small bite appears. Eye protection is essential from now on
Partial phaseThe bite grows for about an hour. The light turns strange and sharper, and the temperature starts to drop
Just before totalityThin crescents of light appear in the shadows of trees, and the last beads of sunlight shine through the valleys of the Moon
TotalityThe sky darkens, the corona appears around the black Moon, planets and bright stars can show and the horizon glows with a sunset colour all around
Just after totalityA flash of light, called the diamond ring, signals the end. Put your glasses back on
Partial phase againThe Moon moves away and normal daylight returns over the next hour

Many people say that the few minutes of totality are emotional, even for people who know the science. Take a moment to look around: the colours of the sky, the animals and the reactions of the people near you are part of the experience.

Weather and clouds on the morning of the eclipse

August is the dry season in northern Morocco, and mornings on the coast are usually mild, with a sea breeze. Clouds or morning haze are still possible, and no forecast can tell you months in advance. Look at the forecast a few days before, and be ready to drive to a clearer spot if you can. A private driver helps because you can decide in the morning and move quickly. The interior of Morocco is much hotter in August, so it is a good idea to stay near the coast or in the mountains of the Rif for the days around the eclipse.

Eclipse safety: how to watch without hurting your eyes

  • Use certified eclipse glasses that meet the ISO 12312-2 standard, and check that they are not scratched.
  • Never use ordinary sunglasses. They do not protect your eyes.
  • Use a certified solar filter on cameras, telescopes and binoculars. Never look through them without one.
  • Look at the Sun without protection only during totality, when the Sun is fully covered, and put your glasses back on the moment the first bright light returns.
  • Do not rely on your phone camera alone. Point it at the Sun without a filter and you can damage the sensor.
  • Protect yourself from the heat. August mornings can already be hot, so bring water, a hat and sun cream.

Safety rules matter more than the best view. If you are not sure about your equipment, watch with your eyes and enjoy the show without a camera.

How to plan your eclipse trip to Morocco

Hotels, flights and transport in the north of Morocco will be in high demand around 2 August 2027, so book early. Travellers from Spain can reach Tangier by ferry from Tarifa or Algeciras, and international flights arrive in Tangier, Casablanca and Marrakech. A private driver and guide makes the day easier because you can leave very early, avoid traffic and reach a good viewing point in time.

Eclipse photography tips

  • Practise before the day with the Moon and your settings.
  • Use a tripod and a certified filter for every phase except totality.
  • Take a wide shot too, to capture the landscape, the sky and the people around you.
  • Do not spend all of totality behind the camera. A few minutes pass very fast, so look up.
  • Charge your batteries and bring spare memory cards.

Solar eclipse 2027 in Morocco: frequently asked questions

When is the next total solar eclipse in Morocco?

The total solar eclipse of 2 August 2027 is the next one that crosses northern Morocco. Totality is visible along a narrow path that includes Tangier, and the rest of the country sees a partial eclipse.

Where can I see the 2027 solar eclipse in Morocco?

Totality passes over the far north of Morocco, including Tangier and the area around Tetouan. To be sure of the full effect, stay inside the path of totality, check an official eclipse map before you book and avoid the edges of the path.

How long will totality last in Tangier?

Most eclipse guides give about 4 minutes and 50 seconds of totality in Tangier, with small differences between sources. The longest totality of this eclipse, about 6 minutes and 23 seconds, is in Egypt, not in Morocco.

What time is the 2027 solar eclipse in Morocco?

The eclipse takes place in the morning, with the greatest eclipse in Tangier around mid-morning local time, close to 9:45. Morocco uses GMT+1 in August, so check our guide to the time in Morocco and confirm the exact times on an eclipse timetable for your location.

Can I see the eclipse from Marrakech?

Marrakech is outside the path of totality, so you will see a partial eclipse, where the Moon covers part of the Sun. It is interesting, but it is not the same as totality. For the total eclipse, travel to the north of Morocco.

Is it safe to look at a solar eclipse?

Never look at the Sun without proper protection. Use eclipse glasses that meet the ISO 12312-2 standard, or a certified solar filter on cameras and binoculars. Only during the few minutes of total eclipse, when the Sun is fully covered, can you look without a filter.

Do ordinary sunglasses work for an eclipse?

No. Ordinary sunglasses, even very dark ones, do not protect your eyes from the Sun. Use certified eclipse glasses and check them for damage before you use them.

What is the difference between a solar eclipse and a lunar eclipse?

In a solar eclipse the Moon passes between the Sun and the Earth and blocks the Sun. In a lunar eclipse the Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon and its shadow falls on the Moon. A solar eclipse is visible only along a narrow path, and a lunar eclipse is visible from half of the planet.

What was the last solar eclipse?

The most recent total solar eclipse took place on 12 August 2026 and crossed Greenland, Iceland and Spain. The next total one for this region is the eclipse of 2 August 2027, and it crosses the Strait of Gibraltar, northern Morocco and North Africa.

How should I plan a trip to see the eclipse in Morocco?

Book flights, rooms and transport early, because the north of Morocco will be busy. Choose a base inside the path, plan to be in position well before the start and keep a backup plan for clouds. We can organise a private tour that includes Tangier and Chefchaouen.
