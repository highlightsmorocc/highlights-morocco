The short answer

A riad is a traditional Moroccan house built around an inner courtyard or garden, usually with a fountain, a roof terrace and only a few rooms. In Marrakech, most riads are in the old medina and work as small guesthouses or boutique hotels, with a quiet, private atmosphere and walking distance to the souks and the main square. Choose a riad for character and calm, and a hotel for a big pool, parking and easy access by car. On our private tours we book riads that match your budget and style.

A stay in a riad is one of the most memorable parts of a trip to Marrakech, but the word can be confusing, because it is used for a building, for a guesthouse and sometimes for a luxury hotel. This guide explains what a riad is, how it differs from a hotel, where to stay, what to expect, how pools and heating work, how to choose a good one and how to book. What does riad mean?

The word riad comes from the Arabic for gardens. In traditional architecture, a riad is a house with an interior garden or courtyard, often planted with orange or lemon trees, with a fountain in the centre. The rooms open onto galleries around the courtyard, and the outside walls have few windows, which keeps the house private and cool. A similar house without a garden is called a dar. Today, in Marrakech, the word riad usually means a restored courtyard house that welcomes guests.

How a riad is built

  • The courtyard: the heart of the house, open to the sky, with a fountain, plants and seating.
  • The galleries: arcades around the courtyard on the ground floor and the upper floors.
  • The rooms: they face inward, with doors and windows opening onto the courtyard.
  • The roof terrace: a place for breakfast, sunset and views of the medina and the Atlas.
  • The decoration: zellige tiles, carved plaster, painted wood and lanterns.
  • The plunge pool: a small pool in some courtyards, for cooling off.

Many riads are old family homes that were restored with local craftsmen. If you like traditional architecture, a riad is also a small museum of Moroccan crafts.

There is no right answer. Many travellers stay in a riad for the medina days and in a hotel or a desert camp for other parts of the trip. For the Sahara part, see our guide to the luxury Morocco tours.

Best areas for a riad in Marrakech

  • Near Jemaa el-Fnaa: the heart of the medina, lively, close to everything; see our Jemaa el-Fnaa guide.
  • Mouassine and the north medina: a quieter area with cafes and crafts shops.
  • The Kasbah and Bahia area: near the palaces; see our Bahia Palace guide.
  • The Mellah: the old Jewish quarter, calmer and authentic.
  • The edge of the medina: easier for taxis, still within walking distance of the sights.

If you arrive with a lot of luggage or at night, choose a riad close to a gate where taxis can stop, and ask the riad to send someone to meet you.

What to expect when you stay in a riad

  • Warm hospitality: you are welcomed with mint tea and shown the house.
  • Breakfast: Moroccan bread, jam, olive oil, eggs and tea, often on the terrace.
  • Dinner: many riads cook a tagine or couscous for guests if you ask in advance.
  • A porter: someone to carry your bags through the lanes.
  • Quiet: the courtyard is calm, even if the lanes are busy.
  • Stairs: many riads have steep stairs and no lift; tell them if mobility is an issue.
  • Character: each riad is different, with its own style and its own quirks.

For the tea ritual that welcomes you, read our guide to Moroccan mint tea.

Pools, heating and comfort

Many travellers expect a swimming pool, but in a riad it is more often a small plunge pool in the courtyard, and it may be unheated. In summer, it is welcome after a hot day, and in winter it is rarely used. Heating also varies: courtyard houses stay cool in summer, but can feel chilly on winter nights, so ask about heaters and blankets if you travel between November and March. Air conditioning is useful from June to September. Check the weather in our Marrakech weather guide before you choose.

What decides the price of a riad

Riad prices vary a lot, so we do not quote a fixed number here. The main factors are the season, with spring, autumn and the holidays the busiest, the location in the medina, the size of the room, the comfort level, the facilities such as a pool or a spa, and whether breakfast and transfers are included. Booking early gives more choice. On our private tours, the riads are included in your quote, so you see the full price in one place.

How to choose a good riad

  • Location: close to a gate if you have luggage, or in the heart of the medina if you want to walk everywhere.
  • Reviews: read recent comments about cleanliness, service and noise.
  • Room position: ask for a room on the courtyard or away from the street.
  • Heating and cooling: check heating for winter and air conditioning for summer.
  • Pool and terrace: check if the pool is heated and if the terrace is open to guests.
  • Breakfast and dinner: ask what is included and if dinner is possible.
  • Pick-up: ask if the riad can arrange a transfer from the airport or the station.

Arriving at a riad

Because many lanes are narrow, your taxi may stop at a gate or a square, and a porter from the riad will meet you. Send the riad your arrival time and your phone number in advance, and keep the address in Latin letters and in Arabic. Do not follow strangers who offer to lead you; wait for the person from the riad. If you arrive by private transfer, our driver coordinates with the riad.

Riads with children

A riad can be a lovely family stay, with a courtyard and a pool, but check the safety first. Open water in a courtyard, steep stairs and low railings may be a concern for young children. A private riad for your family, with the whole house, gives you more space and privacy. Tell us the ages of the children, and we recommend a riad or a hotel that suits your family.

A day in a riad: how it feels

You wake up in a quiet room, with light coming from the courtyard, and the sound of a fountain. Breakfast is served on the terrace, with a view over the roofs of the medina and, on a clear day, the snow on the Atlas in winter. You go out to the souks, a palace or a cooking class, and return in the afternoon to rest in the courtyard with a glass of mint tea. At sunset, you climb back to the terrace, and in the evening you either eat in the riad, which is often a highlight, or walk to the main square for dinner. At night, the house is calm again.

Common mistakes when booking a riad

  • Not checking the location: a riad deep in the lanes can be hard to find at night; ask how to reach it.
  • Expecting hotel services: there is no reception desk all night and no lift in many riads.
  • Ignoring winter: unheated courtyards can be cold; check the heating.
  • Booking only by photos: read recent reviews about noise and cleanliness.
  • Forgetting arrival details: send your arrival time and phone number.
  • Choosing the lowest price only: a very cheap riad may be far from the centre or may lack basic comfort.

Tips for staying in a riad

  • Book early: the best riads fill up in spring and autumn.
  • Share your arrival time: so the porter can meet you.
  • Pack light: a small bag is easier in the lanes.
  • Ask about noise: the call to prayer is early; earplugs can help.
  • Keep cash: for tips to the porter and the staff.
  • Respect the house: be quiet in the courtyard at night.

What is a riad in Marrakech: frequently asked questions

What is a riad in Morocco?

A riad is a traditional Moroccan house built around an inner courtyard or garden, often with a fountain, plants and a roof terrace. Many riads in Marrakech are now small guesthouses or boutique hotels with just a few rooms.

What does the word riad mean?

Riad comes from the Arabic word for gardens. In architecture it describes a house with a central courtyard garden, and in Marrakech people use the word for a guesthouse in an old courtyard house.

Is a riad a hotel?

Not exactly. A riad is a type of building, and many riads work as small hotels or guesthouses. Compared with a large hotel, a riad has fewer rooms, a more personal service and a traditional layout, but fewer facilities.

Where are riads in Marrakech?

Most riads are inside the old medina, in narrow lanes near the souks, Jemaa el-Fnaa, the Bahia Palace and the Kasbah. Large hotels and resorts are usually outside the medina, in areas such as Gueliz and Hivernage.

Is it better to stay in a riad or a hotel in Marrakech?

Choose a riad for atmosphere, a quiet courtyard and walking distance to the medina. Choose a hotel if you want a big pool, a lift, parking, a gym and easier access by car. Some travellers combine both during a trip.

Do riads have swimming pools?

Some do, but many have a small plunge pool in the courtyard rather than a swimming pool, and these are often unheated. If a pool matters to you, ask about its size and whether it is heated.

Are riads cold in winter?

They can be. Courtyard houses stay cool in summer but can feel chilly on winter nights. Ask about heating in the rooms and the common areas before you book between November and March.

Are riads noisy?

The medina is calm at night inside most riads, but you may hear the call to prayer in the early morning and the activity of the lanes. Ask for a room away from the street or the courtyard if you are a light sleeper.

How much does a riad cost in Marrakech?

Prices vary a lot by season, location, size and comfort level, from simple guesthouses to luxury riads. We do not quote fixed rates here; on our private tours we book riads that match your budget and include them in your free quote.

Can a taxi take me to the door of a riad?

Usually not. Many lanes in the medina are too narrow for cars, so you walk the last part, and the riad often sends a porter to carry your luggage. Tell the riad your arrival time in advance.
