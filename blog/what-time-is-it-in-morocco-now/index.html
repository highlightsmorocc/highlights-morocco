Morocco time zone: GMT+1 all year

Morocco is on GMT+1, also written UTC+1. Until 2018 the country moved its clocks forward in spring and back in autumn, like Europe. In October 2018 the government decided to keep GMT+1 all year, which is why many people call it permanent summer time. The country has only one time zone, including the south, so you do not change your watch when you travel from the north to the Sahara.

Many commentators linked the 2018 decision to a wish to stay closer to the working hours of Morocco's European partners, while the Ramadan exception keeps the day closer to the Sun during the fasting month. Whatever the reason, the result is simple for travellers: one time zone, no spring and autumn changes and one rule to remember for Ramadan.

The Ramadan time change in Morocco

During the holy month of Ramadan, Morocco moves its clocks back one hour, so the country is on GMT+0. When Ramadan ends, the clocks move forward again to GMT+1. The dates follow the Islamic calendar, which moves about ten or eleven days earlier each year, and Ramadan 2027 is expected to start in early February. The exact days depend on the moon, and the government confirms them a few days before.

  • Phones and computers update automatically if the date is set to automatic.
  • Flights, trains and tours follow the local time on the day, so check your tickets.
  • Opening hours change in Ramadan, with shorter hours in the day and busy evenings.
  • Our tours continue in Ramadan, and we adapt the timings of meals and stops.

How to convert Morocco time quickly

  • Step 1: find out whether Morocco is on GMT+1 or, during Ramadan, on GMT+0.
  • Step 2: find the GMT offset of your city today, remembering that it changes if your country uses daylight saving time.
  • Step 3: subtract the two offsets. The result is the number of hours between you and Morocco.
  • Example: if you are in New York in January (GMT-5), Morocco is 6 hours ahead, so 9:00 in New York is 15:00 in Morocco.
  • Example: if you are in Madrid in July (GMT+2), Morocco is 1 hour behind, so 21:00 in Madrid is 20:00 in Morocco.

If you do not want to do the maths, use the six city cards in the live clock above, or ask your phone to add a Moroccan city to its world clock. Setting a world clock for Marrakech or Casablanca is the easiest way to avoid mistakes when you book calls, transfers or flights.

Sunrise and sunset in Morocco

Because Morocco stays on GMT+1 all year, the clock time of sunrise and sunset changes through the seasons. In the middle of summer the Sun rises early in the morning and sets in the evening, and in the middle of winter it rises later and sets earlier. These times matter if you want the sunrise on the dunes or the sunset camel ride.

Opening hours in Morocco

  • Offices and banks: usually Monday to Friday, with a longer break around Friday midday.
  • Shops in the medinas: often open later in the morning and stay open into the evening.
  • Museums and monuments: hours vary, so check them in advance.
  • Restaurants: lunch is usually from midday, and dinner is often later in the evening.
  • During Ramadan: many hours change, and evenings are busy after sunset.

These are general patterns, and individual places set their own hours. Always check before you go, especially on Friday and in Ramadan.

Prayer times and the call to prayer

Muslims pray five times a day, and the times follow the Sun, so they move a little every day and change with the seasons. In towns and cities you will hear the call to prayer from the minarets, which is part of the sound of Morocco. On Friday there is a special midday prayer, and some shops and offices close for a while. Visitors are welcome to walk in the medinas at any time, but it is polite to be quiet and respectful near mosques during prayer. Most mosques in Morocco are not open to non-Muslim visitors, with a few exceptions such as the Hassan II Mosque in Casablanca, which offers guided visits.

Time, flights and jet lag

Morocco is close to Europe, so there is little or no jet lag from Spain, Portugal, France or the United Kingdom, and flights are short, from about two hours from Madrid to about three and a half hours from London. From the east coast of North America, flights take about seven to eight hours, and you arrive five to six hours ahead of your home time. A good habit is to set your phone to the Moroccan time as soon as you board, sleep on the plane if you can and spend your first day outside in the daylight.

  • Arrival day: keep it light, with a walk and an early dinner.
  • First night: go to bed at the local time to reset your body clock.
  • Tours: your guide and driver follow the local time, so plan your alarm accordingly.
  • Phone alarms: check that your phone is on automatic time, especially around Ramadan.

Time in Morocco: frequently asked questions

What time is it in Morocco now?

Morocco uses GMT+1 (UTC+1) all year, so the time is the same in Marrakech, Casablanca, Rabat, Fes and Tangier. The live clock at the top of this page shows the exact time in Morocco right now. During Ramadan the country moves back to GMT+0.

What time zone is Morocco in?

Morocco is on GMT+1, which is the Africa/Casablanca time zone. The country does not change its clocks for summer and winter, with one exception: it goes back one hour to GMT+0 during the month of Ramadan.

Does Morocco have daylight saving time?

Since 2018 Morocco keeps GMT+1 permanently, so it does not change the clocks in spring and autumn. Instead, it moves the clocks back one hour for Ramadan and forward again when Ramadan ends.

What time is it in Marrakech right now?

It is the same as in the rest of Morocco. Marrakech, Casablanca, Rabat, Fes, Tangier, Agadir and Merzouga all share one time zone, so you never have to change your watch when you travel between cities.

Is Morocco ahead of or behind London?

In winter Morocco is one hour ahead of London, because London is on GMT+0 and Morocco is on GMT+1. In summer the two have the same time, because London moves to GMT+1. During Ramadan, Morocco is on GMT+0.

Is Morocco the same time as Spain?

In winter Morocco and Spain have the same time. In summer Spain moves one hour ahead, so Morocco is one hour behind Spain. During Ramadan, Morocco is two hours behind Spain in summer and one hour behind in winter.

What is the time difference between Morocco and New York?

Morocco is 6 hours ahead of New York in winter and 5 hours ahead in summer, when the United States is on daylight saving time. The difference can change by an hour for a few weeks in spring and autumn because the dates of the clock changes are different.

Does the time change during Ramadan in Morocco?

Yes. Morocco moves its clocks back one hour for Ramadan, so the country is on GMT+0 during the holy month, and moves them forward again when Ramadan ends. Phones and computers update automatically.

What are the working hours in Morocco?

Many offices work from Monday to Friday, with a longer break around Friday midday. Shops in the medinas often open later in the morning and stay open into the evening. During Ramadan opening hours change. Always check the hours of a place before you go.

How does the time zone affect a desert tour?

Sunrise and sunset change through the year, so the best time for the sunset camel ride and the sunrise over the dunes also changes. We plan your departure time around the daylight in your season.
