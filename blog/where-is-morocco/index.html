The short answer

Morocco is a country in North Africa, in the north-west corner of the African continent. It has coasts on the Atlantic Ocean and the Mediterranean Sea, it borders Algeria to the east and Mauritania to the south, and it is only about 14 kilometres from Spain across the Strait of Gibraltar. The capital is Rabat, the largest city is Casablanca and the most visited city is Marrakech. Morocco is in Africa, not in Europe, and it uses GMT+1 as its time zone.

If you are planning a trip, you probably want to know more than a dot on the map: how far Morocco is from Europe, which countries are next door, where the desert and the mountains are, and how long it takes to get from one city to another. This guide answers all of that with two maps and a few simple tables. When you want help planning, message us on WhatsApp and we plan it for you.

Morocco on the world map

On the world map, look at the north-western corner of Africa, where the Atlantic Ocean meets the Mediterranean Sea. That is Morocco. It is at roughly 32 degrees north and 6 degrees west, which puts it at about the same latitude as the southern United States, and at about the same longitude as Portugal and the United Kingdom. This is why Morocco has such a varied climate, with snow in the mountains, a green coast and a desert in the south-east.

Is Morocco in Africa, Europe or the Arab world?

Morocco is in Africa. At the narrowest point of the Strait of Gibraltar the distance to Spain is only about 14 kilometres, so from Tangier you can see the coast of Europe on a clear day, but the country is part of the African continent. Culturally Morocco is also part of the Arab world and the Maghreb, together with Algeria and Tunisia, and it has strong Amazigh (Berber) roots, Andalusian influence and a long history of trade with Europe and sub-Saharan Africa.

The High Atlas is the backbone of the country, and its highest peak, Toubkal, reaches 4,167 metres, which makes it the highest mountain in North Africa. South of the Atlas, the land dries out into the valleys of the Draa and the Ziz and the dunes of the Sahara. To the north, the Middle Atlas and the Rif mountains catch the rain and the snow. Morocco and Spain: the Strait of Gibraltar

The Strait of Gibraltar is the narrow sea between Morocco and Spain, and it joins the Atlantic Ocean with the Mediterranean. At the narrowest point it is only about 14 kilometres wide, and on a clear day you can see the hills of Spain from Tangier and the mountains of Morocco from Tarifa. Cap Spartel, a headland west of Tangier with a lighthouse, is often described as the place where the two seas meet. Ferries cross every day, which is why many travellers combine a trip to southern Spain with a few days in Morocco. This is also why the north of Morocco is on the path of the total solar eclipse of 2 August 2027, which you can read about in our guide to the solar eclipse 2027 in Morocco.

Why the location makes Morocco special

Morocco sits where Africa, Europe and the Atlantic meet, and that shows in everything: the food, the architecture, the languages and the landscape. It is a short flight from Europe, so a long weekend is possible, and it is big enough for a three-week journey. In a few days you can walk through a thousand-year-old medina, drive over the highest mountains of North Africa, taste Atlantic fish, sleep in a desert camp and watch the sun set over the ocean. For travellers, that variety is the reason to come, and the reason to take your time.

How to get to Morocco

  • By air: the main airports are Marrakech, Casablanca, Fes, Tangier, Agadir and Rabat, with direct flights from many European cities. Flights take about two hours from Madrid and about three and a half hours from London.
  • By ferry: fast ferries cross from Tarifa in Spain to Tangier in about an hour, and from Algeciras to Tangier Med.
  • From North America: flights to Casablanca take roughly seven to eight hours from the east coast.
  • Entry rules: they depend on your nationality, so check the official requirements before you travel.

Once you arrive, a private driver is the easiest way to see several regions in a few days. See our guide to the best time to visit Morocco, the time in Morocco and Morocco tour costs.

Where is Morocco: frequently asked questions

Where is Morocco located?

Morocco is a country in North Africa, in the north-west corner of the African continent. It has coasts on the Atlantic Ocean to the west and the Mediterranean Sea to the north, and it borders Algeria to the east and Mauritania to the south.

Is Morocco in Africa or Europe?

Morocco is in Africa. It is only about 14 kilometres from Spain across the Strait of Gibraltar, so Europe is visible from the northern coast on a clear day, but the country belongs to the African continent.

Which countries border Morocco?

Morocco borders Algeria to the east and Mauritania to the south. Spain has two small territories on the Moroccan coast, Ceuta and Melilla, and also lies across the Strait of Gibraltar to the north.

How far is Morocco from Spain?

At the narrowest point, the Strait of Gibraltar is about 14 kilometres wide. Fast ferries cross from Tarifa in Spain to Tangier in about an hour, and from Algeciras to Tangier Med in a similar time.

What is the capital of Morocco?

The capital of Morocco is Rabat. The largest city and the main business centre is Casablanca, and Marrakech is the most visited city for tourism.

Where is Casablanca in Morocco?

Casablanca is on the Atlantic coast in the north-west of Morocco, about 90 kilometres south-west of Rabat, and about 240 kilometres north of Marrakech.

Where is the Sahara desert in Morocco?

The Moroccan Sahara is in the south-east of the country, close to the Algerian border. The best-known dunes are Erg Chebbi at Merzouga, roughly 560 kilometres from Marrakech, and the dunes near Zagora and M'Hamid.

How do I get to Morocco?

Most travellers fly to Marrakech, Casablanca, Fes, Tangier or Agadir, and there are direct flights from many European cities. You can also take a ferry from Spain to Tangier. Check the entry rules for your nationality before you travel.

What is the time zone in Morocco?

Morocco is on GMT+1 all year, except during Ramadan, when it moves back to GMT+0. See our live clock and guide to the time in Morocco.

What is the best time to visit Morocco?

Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the most comfortable for most trips. Summer is very hot inland and in the desert, and winter is cold in the mountains and at night in the Sahara. See our best time to visit Morocco guide.
